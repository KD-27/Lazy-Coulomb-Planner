# Controlled benchmark, planner v2.0.0

Planner-only comparison of the Lazy Coulomb Planner (LCP) with NavFn, Smac 2D
and Theta* on generated maps, varying one factor at a time around a base map
(50 m square, 10 % occupied, random circles). Run on 15 November 2026, 01:00 to
01:47, on the tagged planner `v2.0.0`.

## Setup

- 18 maps: obstacle density 0 to 30 % (8 maps), map size 10 to 200 m (5, sharing
  the base), obstacle shape (circle, square, concave U), corridor width between
  4 m blocks (0.8 to 3 m). Generated by `benchmark/generate_map.py` with seed 1;
  `maps/manifest.csv` has the arguments and a checksum of every map.
- 100 random start/goal pairs per map (fixed seed, at least 3 m apart, on cells
  with cost below 210). Every pair is planned 5 times by every planner; the time
  used is the median of the five, as reported by `planner_server`.
- Costmap: static map plus inflation, robot radius 0.22 m, inflation radius
  0.7 m, cost scaling 3.0. All planners use their Nav2 defaults. `LCP_lazy` is
  LCP with the grid fallback switched off. Path reuse is off for both LCP
  entries, since the benchmark measures planning from scratch.
- A pair "has a route" by ground truth computed from the map
  (`benchmark/routability.py`), not by any planner's word. A path counts as
  solved only if it never touches a cell with cost 253 or more, that is, a cell
  on which the robot's body would be in contact with an obstacle.
- Machine: Intel Core i7-13620H, 15 GB RAM, Ubuntu 24.04, ROS 2 Jazzy,
  Nav2 1.3.13.

Reproduce: `benchmark/run_suite.py benchmark/suites/controlled.yaml <out>`,
then `benchmark/routability.py <out>` and `benchmark/report_suite.py <out>`.

## Findings

**Speed in open space.** LCP's median planning time against the fastest grid
planner on the same map:

| Obstacle density (50 m map) | 0 % | 2.5 % | 5 % | 10 % | 15 % | 20 % | 25 % | 30 % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LCP (ms) | 0.18 | 0.55 | 0.85 | 1.41 | 1.83 | 2.42 | 7.58 | 15.96 |
| Fastest grid planner (ms) | 9.61 | 10.78 | 12.83 | 14.25 | 14.94 | 13.78 | 13.31 | 13.75 |
| LCP is faster by | 53× | 20× | 15× | 10× | 8× | 6× | 1.8× | 0.9× |

**Crossover.** LCP stops being faster between 25 % and 30 % obstacle density on
this map type. Grid planners' time barely depends on density; LCP's grows with
it, steeply once the lazy search starts handing over to the grid fallback.

**Speed grows with map size** (10 % density):

| Map size | 10 m | 25 m | 50 m | 100 m | 200 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LCP (ms) | 0.26 | 0.66 | 1.41 | 2.38 | 4.46 |
| Fastest grid planner (ms) | 0.77 | 3.85 | 14.25 | 42.65 | 135.32 |
| LCP is faster by | 3× | 6× | 10× | 18× | 30× |

LCP's time grows roughly in proportion to the side of the map, the grid
planners' roughly with its area. On the 200 m map Smac 2D also fails 42 of 100
pairs ("exceeded maximum iterations", its default limit).

**LCP solves every routable pair on all 18 maps with a clean path,** and
correctly refuses the 8 pairs that have no route. The grid planners do not: on
dense maps NavFn, Smac 2D and Theta* return paths that pass through cells where
the robot would touch an obstacle (at 25 % density 19, 20 and 17 of 100 pairs).
Smac 2D and Theta* also return such a path for 6 of the 8 pairs with no route.

**How much the fallback does.** The lazy search alone (`LCP_lazy`) solves all
pairs up to 10 % density, 92 of 100 at 20 %, 72 at 25 % and 52 of 94 at 30 %. On
the concave U-shaped obstacles it solves 53 of 98. The grid fallback covers the
rest, at the cost of time: this is why LCP's median rises from 2.4 ms at 20 % to
16 ms at 30 %, and to 12.5 ms on the U shapes.

**Corridors.** Between 4 m blocks LCP is 1.5× faster than the fastest grid
planner at 0.8 m corridors and 7.5× at 3 m. Smac 2D solves only 54 of 100 pairs
cleanly at 0.8 m.

**Path quality.** LCP paths are 0.5 to 5 % shorter than NavFn's. They run closer
to obstacles: the average costmap cost along an LCP path is 20 on the base map
against 0.4 for NavFn, 6 for Smac 2D and 3 for Theta*.

## Limits of this dataset

- **One map per configuration.** Each density, size and shape was generated with
  a single seed. A paper needs several seeds per configuration to put error bars
  on these numbers, especially near the crossover.
- **Worst-case time.** LCP's slowest plan is 531 ms on the 200 m map and 94 ms on
  the 100 m map, both from the grid fallback searching a very large map. The
  95th percentile there is 14 ms and 7 ms.
- **Default settings for the other planners.** Smac 2D's failures on the 200 m
  map come from its default iteration limit and would likely go away with a
  higher one, at more time.
- **The contact-cell finding depends on the robot radius.** With a smaller robot
  the grid planners' paths would be clean more often.
